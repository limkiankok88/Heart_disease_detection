Heart Disease: Myth vs Fact

10 common misconceptions — and the facts that could save a life. Print & keep handy.
#1
Cause of death worldwide (WHO)
90%
Of cases tied to lifestyle, not genes
1 in 2
Adults 20+ have cardiovascular disease
01
MythHeart disease is a man's disease.
FactIt kills more women than men globally. Women often have subtler symptoms and higher post-attack mortality.
02
MythI'm too young to worry about it.
FactArtery plaque can begin in childhood. Obesity and type-2 diabetes are driving risk younger than ever.
03
MythHigh blood pressure has obvious symptoms.
FactIt's the "silent killer" — most people feel nothing until a heart attack or stroke. Get checked regularly.
04
MythHeart attacks always cause crushing chest pain.
FactMany — women, seniors, diabetics — feel only fatigue, shortness of breath, nausea, or jaw/back pain.
05
MythNo family history means I'm safe.
Fact90% of risk is lifestyle-driven: diet, smoking, inactivity, blood pressure. Genes matter, but aren't destiny.
06
MythBeing thin means my heart is healthy.
FactSkinny people can have high cholesterol, hypertension, or diabetes. Everyone needs screening & exercise.
07
MythA heart attack = sudden cardiac arrest.
FactHeart attack = blocked blood flow (plumbing). Cardiac arrest = faulty heart rhythm (electrical). Different events.
08
MythA daily drink protects the heart.
FactNo alcohol level is truly heart-safe. Even moderate intake raises blood pressure, AFib, and stroke risk.
09
MythSupplements prevent heart disease.
FactNo pill — vitamins, fish oil, CoQ10 — is proven to prevent it. Some can harm. Food, not capsules, protects you.
10
MythDiabetes meds fully protect my heart.
FactMedication helps, but diabetics stay at higher cardiovascular risk. Lifestyle changes remain essential.

Call emergency services immediately if you notice:

Chest pressure or pain · pain spreading to arm/jaw/back · sudden shortness of breath · cold sweat, nausea, or lightheadedness · unexplained extreme fatigue. Every minute counts — do not drive yourself.

This sheet is for education only and is not medical advice. Always consult a healthcare professional.
